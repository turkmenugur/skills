# Retention Psychology — Three Engagement Mechanisms

Source: Tim Gabe, "How To Scientifically Design Addictive Apps." Read this when designing progression, rewards, or anything meant to bring users back. Pair it with `gamification-that-works.md`, which documents where these mechanics backfire — use both together and design for genuine value, not compulsion.

> Note on ethics: these mechanisms are powerful and can be misused. Build them to reward real progress and real user benefit. Avoid mechanics a user cannot pause, influence, or escape — they harm users and are increasingly flagged by researchers and regulators (see the streak-trap section in `gamification-that-works.md`).

Surface gamification (streaks, badges, leaderboards) usually fails to retain. Top apps embed engagement into the **architecture** of the product via three psychological mechanisms.

## Mechanism 1 — The Craving Machine

**Foundation:** variable-ratio reinforcement (B.F. Skinner). Rewards on an unpredictable schedule trigger a dopamine chase — a "craving" that keeps the brain seeking the next hit.

- **Inject controlled surprise.** Keep most of the reward system predictable and transparent so users feel in control, then sprinkle unpredictability (surprise milestones, bonuses) to delight them off-guard.
- **Track one centralized progression system.** One high-fidelity metric to obsess over (Finch's 6-trait profile, a single visible match rank) beats 20 scattered badges.
- **Maintain an evolution cycle.** Keep one element constantly evolving beyond the user's direct control, so they feel compelled to check what happened.

## Mechanism 2 — The Infinite Game

**Foundation:** loss aversion. Losing something hurts about twice as much as gaining the equivalent feels good.

- **Audit and eliminate "done" states.** If a user can fully "complete" your app, you've capped retention. There should always be *more*.
- **Build compounding streaks** that unlock tangible, accumulating value rather than a counter that just resets to zero. Missing a day should risk earned assets, not only a number. (But always give the user a way to pause/protect it — see ethics note.)
- **Periodic resets with retained status.** Reset the active ladder (e.g. competitive rank) but preserve hard-earned permanent status, cosmetics, and lifetime metrics.

## Mechanism 3 — The Invisible Scoreboard

**Foundation:** social comparison theory. Humans instinctively compare to peers. Without social visibility a user can quit privately; with it, quitting becomes a public admission.

- **Turn personal goals into status symbols.** When progression ties to social status, engagement becomes *identity* — and people rarely walk away from identity.
- **Prioritize community over isolated features.** AI can generate a plan or a leaderboard, but not genuine human connection. Bring parasocial elements, instructor-led dynamics, or accountability into the core loop.
- **Design metrics as a social mirror.** A stats dashboard should make users think about how they stack up against friends or peers, not read like a clinical report.

## TL;DR

1. **Craving Machine** gets users in the door (unpredictable rewards keep them chasing).
2. **Infinite Game** keeps them running (loss aversion + no end-state).
3. **Invisible Scoreboard** makes standing still feel public (engagement becomes status).

> Core truth: it's not *what* these apps reward, it's *how* they structure the reward. The gap between surface gamification and a real retention system is the gap between decoration and architecture.
